Agent Based Simulation of Carbon Emissions Trading Market 
 
Fatma Mutlu, Nilgun Fescioglu-Unver 
TOBB University of Economics and Technology, Industrial Engineering Department, Ankara, Turkey 
 
Abstract--In this research we examine the supply and 
demand relationship in a carbon emissions trading market by 
using agent based system simulation.  An agent based system is 
a computational system that simulates the behaviors of 
autonomous agents. We model different nations which take 
place in the carbon trading mark et as autonomous agents, and 
these agents act as buyers or sellers in the same trading market. 
A market-director agent directs the trading between the buyer 
and seller agents and enables a market mechanism with 
dynamic auction. This study an alyzes the changes in carbon 
price and the total carbon reduction amount under several 
different demand scenarios. We used JADE (Java Based Agent 
Development Environment) as the simulation environment. 
 
I. INTRODUCTION 
 
This paper presents an application of agent based 
simulation to analyze the carbon trading market. Agent based 
simulation is a tool to model and analyze the behavior of 
complex systems composed of autonomous agents. Agents 
act in an environment, which has policies and interaction 
protocols that design the communication between agents. 
Agents may have different behaviors, and strategic goals to 
achieve. Agents may learn throughout their lifetime, use their 
knowledge to forecast future ev ents and adapt their behavior 
accordingly. Energy manageme nt related systems involve 
different interacting components, which can be modeled 
using multi agent systems and the behavior of these systems 
under different scenarios can be analyzed using agent based 
simulation. 
Kyoto Protocol aims to reduce the greenhouse gas 
emissions to prevent the climate change. Kyoto Protocol 
Annex-1 countries are expected to develop clean 
manufacturing processes to make this reduction. Kyoto 
protocol also brings three flexibility mechanisms to help 
countries meet their reduction commitments. ET (Emission 
Trading) is one of these mechanisms, and it helps Annex 1 
regions meet their emission reduction target at a reduced cost 
[13]. One type of emissions trading is carbon emission 
trading which is specifically for carbon dioxide. A nation 
buying carbon in the carbon trading market buys the right to 
burn it and a nation selling it gives up the right to burn it [14]. 
Regions such as Former Soviet Union and Eastern Europe 
have surplus carbon credits called “hot air” because their 
emissions are already under the amounts of emissions 
specified in accordance with the Kyoto Protocol.   
This paper aims to analyze the supply and demand 
relationship in the carbon trading market, using agent based 
simulation approach. The paper is organized as follows: 
Section 2 presents a short review of recent agent based 
applications and gives background information on carbon 
trading. Section 3 presents the carbon trading model and 
agent based simulation model. Section 4 introduces several 
scenarios and discusses the model results. Finally, Section 5 
discusses conclusions and future research directions. 
 
II. BACKGROUND 
 
A. Agent Application Examples  
As analyzing dynamically ch anging situations is a 
difficult task, many researchers suggest using agent-based 
simulation approach, which is one of the approaches applied 
to investigate complex dynamics in the studies on economy 
[12]. An agent based system is  a computational system that 
simulates the behaviors of autonomous agents [11]. There are 
a variety of areas where agent based systems are used. Some 
of application areas are as follows: manufacturing, 
scheduling, transportation, supply chain management etc.  
In a dynamic scheduling system, agents are assigned to 
work centers and these agents choose the best dispatching 
rules dynamically [10]. An example is the multi agent 
scheduling system proposed by Glaschenko et al. as a 
solution for taxi companies [6].  
There are also a number of agent based applications about 
traffic control and management. Chen et al. propose a traffic 
management system application, which integrates multi-agent 
systems with mobile agent technology in order to cope with 
uncertainties of the dynamic environment more effectively 
[3]. 
Another area that multi-agent technology is used is 
education. Turgay [16] proposes agent systems with 
intelligence and cooperation features for distance learning. 
Another example is about a distance learning system 
composing of two multi-agent systems which are the higher 
level providing the main functions of the system and the 
lower level being responsible for diagnosing students’ 
conceptions [17]. 
Mizuta et al. [12] developed a framework for agent-based 
simulations and an application simulating a dynamic model 
of international greenhouse gas emissions trading composed 
of heterogeneous and dynamic economic entities. The 
framework proposed is an Artificial Society with Interacting 
Agents (ASIA) providing only simple and fundamental 
functionality for the simulation and this framework is used 
for GHG (Greenhouse Gas) emissions trading.  In their study, 
countries are represented as agents which makes changing the 
behavior of countries easy [12].

compares the outcomes of different trading scenarios and 
states that the potential largen ess of the gains of emission

B. Agent-Based Model 
In this study, JADE (Java Based Agent Development 
Environment), a middleware facilitating the development of 
multi-agent systems, is used. JADE agents execute tasks and 
communicate with each other. [2] 
In the scope of our study we created agents to represent 
supplier regions and buyer region s. One agent is created as 
the director agent and it directs the trading between the 
supplier and buyer agents.  
Agent based simulation was previously used in other 
studies which investigate the carbon trading market. Mizuta 
et al. [12] developed an agent-based system simulation of 
GHG emissions trading market.  In this application there is a 
directing agent (called the COP agent) who manage the trade. 
COP agent tries to find the equilibrium price where the 
demand and supply balance in order to minimize the 
worldwide cost of GHG reduction [12]. Both this application 
and our application are agent-based. However, we use JADE 
in our study and we test different scenarios.

determine demand and supply of emission permits. If a 
country’s marginal abatement co st is lower than the market

carbon at this price level, it will buy emission permits from 
other Annex-1 countries or generate CDM credits by making 
clean development projects with non-Annex 1 countries. If an 
Annex-1 country can reduce its carbon emission more than its 
target level, or if it already emits less carbon than its target 
(means that the country has hot air), then it will sell permits

The trading algorithm starts  when the director agent 
announces the initial market price to suppliers and buyers. 
These suppliers and buyers info rm the director agent about

their supply or demand quantities considering the announced 
market price. Total supply quantities and total demand 
quantities are calculated after all the suppliers and buyers 
inform the director agent. If total supply quantity is less than 
total demand quantity, director agent announces the new 
market price by increasing it an d the scenario continues in a 
similar way. When the total demand is equal to total supply 
amount, the price settles and trading occurs. 
The workings of the algorithm can be better explained by 
an example: Initially all the buyer and supplier agents register 
with the market. Director agent announces the initial market 
price as 1 $/ton C. A buyer country computes the amount of 
Carbon it can reduce by his own means, considering its MAC 
corresponding to this market price. In other words this 
emission reduction is calculated using (1). If the target 
requirement is higher than this reduction, the difference 
between the reduction requirement and domestic reduction is 
attained as the demand of this buyer country. Calculating all 
buyer countries’ demands in a similar way, we determine the 
total demand called TD. A supplier country also determines 
the emission quantity it tends to sell that its MAC 
corresponds to. This quantity is also calculated using (1) 
similar to the calculation of buyer’s emission reduction 
quantity. If this buyer has hot air, the amount of hot air is 
added to its supply quantity. Like calculating the total 
demand, we determine the total supply quantity called TS. If 
TS is greater than equal to TD , trading occurs otherwise we 
increase the market price by 1 $ /ton C. We expect that when 
the market price increases, tota l supply also increases and 
conversely, total demand decreases. 
Equation (2) is used to compute buyer regions’ domestic 
reduction cost and supplier region’s reduction cost. For a 
buyer region, the difference between required reduction and 
domestic reduction (if required reduction is higher than 
domestic reduction) shows the amount of emission reduction 
provided with purchasing permits from supplier regions and 
the total price of this purchase is obtained by multiplying 
amount of this reduction with the current market price. 
For the calculation of the reve nue of a supplier, for both 
the amount of emission it tends to sell and hot air, if it exists, 
we use (3). The profit of the supplier region is obtained by 
subtracting cost from revenue.

V. CONCLUSION 
 
This study presents a multi-agent based simulation model 
of carbon trading market. The paper covers problem 
definition, describes a multi-agent approach to carbon 
trading, and analyzes several scenarios. With the system 
established in the scope of this study, we can examine how 
the demand and supply size changes in accordance with the 
changes in the market price.  
This paper presents the first stage of the study. In this 
stage we developed an agent based simulation model to 
examine the dynamic interactions in the carbon trading 
market. The results of this st udy are not compared to what 
actually happened in the market , and how the price and total 
supply and demand actually changed. Future studies should 
examine the real market s ituation and investigate the 
environmental conditions, which led the market towards the 
final condition. Future work in this area should also 
investigate how the simulated market reaction would change 
if the agents had in telligence, different goals and forecasting 
abilities. 
Multi-agent based systems help to model the interaction 
between distributed independent entities. Technology 
management systems can make use of multi agent systems to 
model the interactions between the actors of an energy-smart 
world.
