A B S T R A C T
This paper aims to address the bidding strategy optimization in the real-time multi-participant electricity
market with short-term load dynamics. In order to avoid the sub-optimal solution and the dependence on
the complete information in traditional mathematical programming methods, an electricity market bidding
strategy optimization algorithm based on deep reinforcement learning (DRL) is developed. While conventional
reinforcement learning algorithms (e.g., Q-learning and deep Q-learning) are only capable of handling simple
problems in discrete state spaces, the proximal policy optimization (PPO) algorithm is implemented in the
bidding strategy optimization since it can optimize the bidding strategy in the continuous action and state
spaces. In order to substantiate the aforementioned perspective, this paper conducts a two-part experimental
study. First, experiments which consider the fixed demand load of market participants show that the developed
method can reach the Nash equilibrium just like the bi-level optimization, and higher profits can be achieved
by adjusting hyperparameters. Then, complex experiments which consider the time-varying demand load verify
the DRL-based electricity market bidding strategy performs better than bi-level optimization-based methods
and increases the profits of generators.
1. Introduction
In order to improve the electricity efficiency and maximize the
social welfare, the electricity market has introduced the competition
mechanism. In particular, the deregulated electric wholesale market
has introduced competition among multiple self-interested market par-
ticipants, i.e., the generation companies (GenCos). In the electricity
clearing process, independent system operators (ISOs) clear the market
based on the supply and demand quotations provided by market par-
ticipants, where GenCos are viewed as agents. As rational participants,
GenCos select the most appropriate bidding strategies to maximize their
profits through cooperation and competition. Generally speaking, the
optimization of electricity market bidding strategy is formulated as a bi-
level mathematical programming, where the upper-level optimization
aims to maximize the profits of individual GenCos and the lower-level
optimization aims to clear the electricity market under the condition of
supply–demand balance of powers. The superiority of this model lies
in its ability to capture the interaction between the strategy making of

For the single-level mathematical programming, some methods in-
cluding the simple method, the big 𝑀 method and the interior-point
method [ 1] have been developed. However, the above methods can
only solve problems with simple objective functions and simple con-
straints. Thus, they are not applicable to the bi-level optimization of
bidding strategy. In the bi-level-based framework, the evaluation of the
upper-level problem depends on the settlement of the lower-level prob-
lem, which makes it difficult and time-consuming for optimizing the
bidding strategy of a GenCo. In fact, many bi-level optimization prob-
lems are non-deterministic polynomial-time hard (NP-hard) [ 2], and
thus, sub-optimal solutions are always obtained. Nevertheless, some
special approaches, such as the traversal algorithm [ 3], the interior-
point method [ 4] and the ergodic method [ 5], have been developed
to obtain appropriate solutions for optimizing the electricity market.

Among these methods, the most popular way is the mathematical
programming methods with equilibrium constraints (MPEC). In this
method, the lower-level problem which is replaced by the Karush–
Kuhn–Tucker (KKT) can be viewed as part of constrained conditions
of the upper-level optimization problem. In this case, the bi-level
optimization is converted to a single-level MPEC model which eases
the upper-level optimization problem. However, the MPEC method has
two limitations. First, the complicated transformation from the lower-
level problem to its KKT conditions always requires the lower-level
formulas to be convex. Otherwise, the global optimal solution of the
bi-level programming cannot be guaranteed. This requirement means
that MPEC cannot be used to non-convex programming. Meanwhile, it
is not realizable to collect the complete information from all GenCos,
which are necessary in optimizing the MPEC model in the real-time
electricity market.
As a model-free machine learning algorithm, reinforcement learning
(RL), which does not require a precise mathematical programming
model, enables agents to optimize the action by using their behavior
and experience feedback in an interactive environment through trial
and error. This motivates many researchers to employ RL in computer
games [6], robotics [7], industrial automation [8–10] and so on. As
one of the earliest RL algorithms, Q-learning which searches discrete
state and action spaces by looking up the table, easily suffers from
the ‘‘curse of dimensionality’’ due to the increase in state or action
spaces [11], which leads to an adverse effect on the feasible action
space, and results in a sub-optimal strategy. In the complex electricity
market environment, the state (e.g., the market clearing price and
dispatch) and the action space of the agent are not only continuous but
also multi-dimensional. DRL, which is popularized by the intelligent
computer program AlphaGo, is an advanced variant of RL to solve
temporal decision problems with partial or hidden information due to
the combination of deep neural network (DNN) [12] and RL. Compared
with the traditional Q-learning algorithm, the advantage of DRL lies in
that it uses DNN to establish the generalized mapping between states
and action-state values (i.e., the Q-function) [13], rather than storing
the specific look-up tables as in Q-learning. Thus, the DRL algorithm
can be applied to continuous environment settings.
Beyond the above success of RL algorithms in various domains, it
is also applied to the sub-domains of the electricity market research,
such as demand-side energy management [14,15] and residential load
control [16,17]. In particular, RL algorithms are also used to optimize
the bidding strategy of the electricity market. For example, [18] em-
ployed a fuzzy Q-learning method to model the GenCo strategic bidding
behaviors. The experiments demonstrate that the proposed fuzzy Q-
learning can accumulate more profits in the long run in comparison
with the conventional Q-learning. However, this method is still con-
fined to the discrete state space and cannot deal with the uncertainties
of the market. [19] employed the deep deterministic policy gradient
(DDPG) to optimize the profit of single agent. The experiments show
that the DDPG obtains higher profits than other methods. However,
it can only optimize the strategy of single agent. [20] developed the
bidding strategy in the day-ahead electricity market. In the proposed
method, a GenCo submits the offer price for the next 24 h in day-ahead
electricity market while it submits the offer price every 15 min to the
real-time electricity market. In this approach, the real-time electricity
market is described as a partially observable Markov decision process
(MDP), which is solved through an RL algorithm [21]. MDP offers
a systematic way to solve multi-stage probabilistic decision-making
problems. In this process, the market participants continuously find the
optimal strategy to maximize the expected reward.
As a prominent approach of deep reinforcement learning, the prox-
imal policy optimization (PPO) [22] algorithm is introduced in this
paper. The PPO algorithm is a kind of two neural networks-based off-
policy method, which has excellent robustness and accuracy. Compared
with MPEC, this paper proposes a PPO-based electricity market bid-
ding strategy algorithm (PPO-EMBSA) by employing RL to handle the
bidding strategy optimization problem in the electricity market. The
clip mechanism [23] of the PPO-EMBSA limits the step size of policy
updating, and prevents instability during the training process. When
the user demand continuously changes, the advantages of PPO-EMBSA
enable it to learn the optimal policy efficiently and to effectively adapt
to the dynamics of the market. The stability is ensured through the
great robustness of PPO-EMBSA. In general, in order to achieve the
supply function equilibrium (SFE) of the electricity market, two kinds
of experiments are accomplished to test the algorithm performance. On
the one hand, the feasibility of the PPO-EMBSA is verified by testing
whether it can approximate the Nash equilibrium (NE) [24] solution
with simple experiments. Furthermore, the hyper-parameter experi-
ment, which is conducted as part of simple experiments, demonstrates
that adjusting the hyper-parameter of the algorithm can lead to higher
profits. On the other hand, the PPO-EMBSA is tested on a more complex
optimization problem model by considering the long-term profit. In
particular, this paper proves the superiority of the PPO-EMBSA by
comparing it with Q-learning, DQN, asynchronous advantage actor-
critic (A3C) and deep deterministic policy gradient (DDPG). The main
contributions and novelties of this paper are summarized as follows.
1. A PPO-EMBSA is proposed to overcome the limitations of the
MPEC-based method in two distinct aspects. First, PPO-EMBSA
does not need to transform the lower-level problem of the bi-
level programming to the corresponding KKT condition. Instead,
it selects bidding actions directly based on a neural network
and continuously optimizes the policy until the NE is approxi-
mated in the training process. Second, agents select appropriate
strategies by PPO-EMBSA in an environment with incomplete in-
formation and optimizes the present strategy for future rewards,
which is superior to the requirement of complete information in
the MPEC-based method.
2. Compared with other RL algorithms, the proposed PPO-EMBSA
tackles the high-dimensional continuous action more appropri-
ately. It employs two neural networks to approximate the action
and the value functions, respectively. This greatly improves the
accuracy and the stability of RL-based electricity market bidding
strategies.
3. Experimental results show that the PPO-EMBSA derives appro-
priate bidding strategies in situations of both fixed demand load
and time-varying load. Then, the experiments also verify that the
proposed method is more competitive than bidding algorithms
based on Q-learning, DQN and A3C. In particular, additional
ablation studies discuss the sensitivity of PPO-EMBSA to some
hyper-parameters. Furthermore, comparative experiments with
DDPG confirm its superior robustness and adaptability in the
electricity market environment.
The rest of this paper is organized as follows. Section 2 introduces
the bidding framework of power producers and formulates the clear-
ing process of market electricity. Section 3 establishes the bidding
strategies of GenCos based on the PPO algorithm. Section 4 simulates
the electricity market and analyzes the results by using different al-
gorithms. Section 5 summarizes this paper and presents the future
work.
2. Bidding framework in the electricity market
This paper employs SFE model [25,26] to construct the market
clearing model, which is commonly utilized in real-time electricity
markets to represent the competitive behavior of GenCos. The market
clearing model interacts with the agents as part of the training process.
For further understanding the background of the electricity market, we
may refer to papers [20,27,28].
The bidding framework of the electricity market consists of two
parts, i.e., the liquidation of competition in the electricity market
(lower-level), and the strategic game between the self-interested market
participants (upper-level).

3.2. The bidding strategy of GenCos with PPO
In this paper, a PPO algorithm is developed to optimize the bidding
strategy of GenCos. The training process of each training round for PPO-
EMBSA is shown in Fig. 3. In the market clearing process, each agent
independently learns its own strategy, and strategies of other agents
are considered as part of the environment. Note that the environment
may not be stable since all agents make the decision independently.
In the experiments, the independent setting of each agent often causes
unstable optimization results, i.e., the experimental results have large
variance. The unstable results of the agents make the decision without
considering the situation of its competitors. This indicates that an agent
may make a wrong judge of the actual situation of electricity market
easily. Thus, a robust algorithm is presented to obtain appropriate
bidding strategies. Comparison experiments show that the PPO always
approximates NE strategies stably and obtains higher profits in the
electricity market.
The behavior of the PPO-EMBSA is analyzed from two aspects:
(1) The strategy derived by the PPO-EMBSA is compared with the
NE strategy derived by the mathematical programming method. If
the PPO-based bidding strategy approaches the NE, a suitable bidding
strategy is found. (2) The introduction of the time-varying load demand
will aggravate the environment instability and affect the algorithm
convergence. In this case, the bidding strategy will be changed along
with the load fluctuation and outputs the profit at each time instant.
The key elements of the electricity market bidding strategy are listed
as follows.
(1) Environment: The environment is represented by the market
clearing process, which is the bi-level optimization model described in
Section 2. The strategies of GenCos are regarded as the inputs of the
environment, and the nodal price and profit are regarded as the next
state and the current reward.
(2) Agent: The agent makes the corresponding action 𝑎𝑡 according
to the state 𝑠𝑡 in RL. In this paper, the GenCo is regarded as the agent
of the PPO-EMBSA to participate in the market clearing process. Note

(2) The sharing of multiple threads introduces the complexity in pa-
rameter updating. When different threads simultaneously update the
same parameter, it may lead to the overwriting by a certain thread and
impacting the convergence of the model.

4.1.2. Influence of the hyper-parameters
In different environments, the hyper-parameters affect the perfor-
mance of RL algorithms greatly. That is to say, the algorithm is very
sensitive to some hyper-parameters. The discount factor 𝛾 is used to
tradeoff the importance between the immediate and future rewards. If𝛾
is close to 0, the agents pay more attention to current rewards, and less
foresight of the future rewards. In this case, the agents may converge to
the bidding strategies with lower profits. If𝛾 is close to 1, the agents pay
more attention to the future rewards, but it does not mean that𝛾 should
be selected as high as possible because of the behavioral uncertainty of
agents. Thus, it is a proper value depending on the experimental results.
The experiments are still conducted on the 3-bus power system. For
the PPO-based bidding strategy with different 𝛾, we perform multiple
experiments and record the average profits. The influence of 𝛾 on
strategy selection is shown in Fig. 7 . When 𝛾 < 0.9, there exists
slight fluctuation of𝛼𝑔𝑡. However, the difference among the convergent
strategies is not obvious, and when 𝛾 > 0.9, the experimental results
are quite different. At the same time, the significant ascent increases
the error, which means the action-value function is unstable. That is
because agents pay more attention to future rewards. The profit of the
