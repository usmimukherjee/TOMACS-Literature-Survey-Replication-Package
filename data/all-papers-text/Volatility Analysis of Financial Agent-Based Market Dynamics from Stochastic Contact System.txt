Abstract A ﬁnancial agent-based time series model is developed and investigated
by the stochastic contact systems. Multicolor contact system, as one of statistical
physics systems, is applied to model a random stock price process for investigating
the ﬂuctuation dynamics of ﬁnancial market. The interaction and dispersal of different
types of investment attitudes in a ﬁnancial market is imitated by viruses spreading
in a multicolor contact system, and we suppose that the investment attitudes of mar-
ket participants contribute to the volatilities of ﬁnancial time series. We introduce a
volatility duration analysis to detect the duration and intensity relationship of time
series for both SSECI and the ﬁnancial model. Furthermore, the empirical research is
also presented to study the nonlinear behaviors of returns for the actual data and the
simulation data.
Keywords Financial agent-based price model · Stochastic contact system · Nonlinear
analysis · V olatility analysis· Statistical physics

returns, volatility clustering, aggregational gaussianity and multifractality ( Bouchaud

Capturing these features of ﬁnancial markets is crucial for many purposes including
but not limited to: derivatives pricing, hedging, forecasting volatility, portfolio man-
agement, regulatory issues, value-at-risk, etc. Some of these approaches come from
the ﬁeld of interacting particle systems ( Fang and Wang 2012 ; Lux and Marchesi
1990; Niu and Wang 2013 ; Plerou et al. 2000 ; Stauffer and Penna 1998 ; Wang and
Wang 2012; Wang et al. 2010 ; Wang et al. 2011 ; Xiao and Wang 2012 ; Zhang and
Wang 2010). Plerou et al. (2000) pointed out that economic systems such as ﬁnancial
markets are similar to physical systems in that they are comprised of a large number
of interacting “agents”. These economic agents are “thinking” units and they interact
in complicated ways. One of the famous model is Stauffer–Penna model ( Stauffer
and Penna 1998 ). They developed a price model by the lattice percolation system,
the local interaction or inﬂuence among traders in a stock market is constructed, and
a cluster of percolation was applied to deﬁne the cluster of traders sharing the same
opinion about the market. Their model was based on the herding effect ( Lux and
Marchesi 1990; Bannerjee 1992; Cont and Bouchaud 2000 ) by which traders follow
trends without considering any economic data. The existence of heavy tail behavior
for the returns is found when the inﬂuence rate of the model is around or at a critical
value.
This current interest in the subject stems from a recent simulation of ﬁnancial mar-
ket dynamics ( Zhang and Wang 2010 ). At the root of that study is a microscopic
model based on the contact process, a model for epidemic spreading in a continuous
time Markov process with state space {0, 1}Zd
, where Zd is d-dimensional integer
lattice. One interpretation of the contact process is often regarded as a crude model
for the spread of a disease. If the state of the process at a given time s is ηs , then
a site x in Zd is healthy if ηs(x) = 0 or infected if ηs(x) = 1. Healthy individu-
als become infected at a rate proportional to the number of the infected neighbors,
and infected individuals recover at a constant rate. A brief introduction of the theory
of contact process could be seen in Liggett (1985) and some generalizations could
be seen in Liggett (1999), Divakaran and Dutta (2007). The ﬁnancial model from
contact process ( Zhang and Wang 2010 ) is able to produce some desirable charac-
teristics of real ﬁnancial markets, including the heavy tail phenomena, the power law
distribution and the long range correlation. In this paper, we present a new ﬁnan-
cial model based on the multicolor contact process (also named multitype contact
process). The multicolor contact process, as its name implies, is different from the
ordinary contact process for it has more than one type of particles in the process. It
is the result of generalizing the state space from {0, 1}Zd
to {0, 1,..., k}Zd
.F o rm o r e
theories of multicolor contact process, one may consider ( Cox and Schinazi 2009 ;
Krone 1999; Liu and Wang 1994 ; Neuhauser 1992). Speciﬁcally in this paper, there
are two types of infected particles, which can be interpreted as a model for the spread-
ing of two kinds of diseases, one heavy ηs(x) = 2 and one slight ηs(x) = 1. They
spread at different rates, proportional to the number of heavily and slightly infected
neighbors respectively, and both types of infected individuals recover at a constant
rate. The interesting part of the rule is that, a heavily infected individual can affect

its slightly infected neighbors, changing them into heavily infected ones, while the
converse is not possible. The ﬁnancial model for the price evolution possesses the
following features: (i) investment attitudes of various levels of strength, which are
referred to strong attitude, weak attitude and neutral attitude respectively represented
by the three types of particles with different spreading rates in the multicolor con-
tact model; (ii) random birth of “well informed” traders (market leaders) with new
attitudes; (iii) dispersal of three types of attitudes at different rates with different inter-
action mechanism. To better understand the spread of a type of attitude in this model,
we can refer to a chartist strategy, since in both cases decisions are based on informa-
tion of market future movements. While a chartist infers this information from past
price movements, an attitude holder in the proposed model relies on certain signals he
receives.
The motivation of proposing this multicolor contact process originates from an
observation of the structure of Chinese stock markets. According to the data from
China Securities Depository and Clearing Company Limited (CSDCC), up to April
2012, of all accounts with stock holding on A-share market, more than 85% are less
than ¥ 100,000 in market value and more than 97 % are less than ¥ 500,000. This
indicates that retail private investors are still the majority in A share market. And
these retail investors have records of their following behavior (Li et al. 2015). Though
both institutional investor and retail investor demonstrate herding behavior in Chi-
nese markets, the latter is much more notorious for a lack of proper information
and are easily inﬂuenced by market sentiment. We believe that the multicolor con-
tact process, with multitype particles, is able to imitate the interaction of retail and
institutional investors to some extent. Just like other numerous agent-based models
emerging in the past two decades (see Chakraborti et al. 2011 ; Samanidou et al. 2007
for a review), which aim at behavioral explanations of the stylized facts from various
ways, the present model intends to offer an alternative perspective of distinguishing
investment attitudes into strong and weak. We hope that this new approach can be
helpful for the understanding of price dynamics in Chinese stock markets to some
extent.
In the present work, the proposed model is applied to investigate the price dynam-
ics with a focus on three types of “stylized facts”. (i) Heavy tails: the distribution
of returns seems to display a power law tail, ﬁnite variance and the normal distrib-
ution; (ii) Aggregational Gaussianity: as one increases the time interval over which
returns are calculated, their distributions look more and more like a normal distribu-
tion; (iii) V olatility clustering: as noted by Mandelbrot (1963), “large changes tend to
be followed by large changes, of either sign, and small changes tend to be followed by
small changes”. Furthermore, we introduce a new analysis method transferring volatil-
ity series into volatility duration series, and apply it to investigate the corresponding
duration patterns. And the comparison between the simulation data and the empirical
data is also exhibited by the volatility duration analysis. For the empirical research,
the daily closing prices of Shanghai Stock Exchange Composite Index (SSECI) from
5th Jan. 1998 to 31th Jan. 2012, and Hang Seng Index (HSI) from 5th Jan. 1998 to
31th Oct. 2011 are selected as the empirical data.

2.1 Description of Multicolor Contact System
In this section, we introduce the mathematical description of the multicolor contact
process, for details see ( Gabaix et al. 2003 ; Liggett 1985 ; Liggett 1999 ; Liu and
Wang 1994; Chen 1992). The multicolor contact process on Zd is a continuous time
Markov process {ηs, s > 0} with infection parameters λ1 and λ2 on the conﬁgu-
ration space {0, 1, 2}Zd
. A conﬁguration ηs is often identiﬁed with two subsets A1
and A2 via A1 ={ x ∈ Zd : ηs(x) = 1} and A2 ={ x ∈ Zd : ηs(x) = 2}
respectively. We can interpret elements in Ai (i = 1, 2) as i type of particles. For
simplicity, we consider the one-dimensional multicolor contact model, the process
evolves according to the following rules: (i) particles die at rate 1; (ii) particles
of type i give birth at rate λi (i = 1, 2); (iii) a new particle from x is sent to
y chosen at random from {y :| y − x|= 1}; (iv) when ηs(y)>η s(x),t h e
birth is suppressed. As a metaphor, the relationship of “tree, bush and grass” can
be applied to describe this dynamic system. In this interpretation, 2 = tree, 1 =
bush and 0 = grass, tree and bush die at the same rate, after death they become
grasses. And they also have different birth rates, if a new tree is born and sent to
the site of a bush, then at that site, a tree grows up and the bush dies. But if a
new bush, is born and sent to the site of a tree, then no action is taken at that site,
the tree is still alive (at each site, only one plant is allowed to grow up). If a new
tree (or bush) is born and sent to the site of a grass, then at that site, a new tree
(or bush) grows up. Next, we introduce the graphical representation of the one-
dimensional multicolor contact model on {0, 1, 2}Z, which is useful to illustrate
and simulate the model. Considering Z × R+ as the space-time, for all x ∈ Z,

2.2 Price Model for a Stock Market
In the present work, we assume that the stock price ﬂuctuations result from the traders’
investment decisions towards the stock market, and suppose that the investment atti-
tudes are represented by the particles of the multicolor contact model. We classify
the attitudes into three categories according to levels of strength: strong attitude, weak
attitude and neutral attitude, corresponding to type 2, type 1 and type 0 particles respec-
tively. This is also reasonable in real world, the investment attitudes in real market are
123

612 D. Xiao et al.
not always of the same, that is, some investors are more conﬁdent in their decisions
while others are not so sure and relatively easy to alter. Now we consider a ﬁnancial
model of auctions for a single stock or stock indexes in a ﬁnancial market. We sup-
pose that strong and weak attitudes coexist in the ﬁnancial market just as two kinds of
viruses in the multicolor contact system. In the model, strong attitude and weak attitude
spread at different propagation rates, and strong attitude holder can affect both weak
attitude holders and neutral ones, while weak attitude holder can only affect neutral
ones but not strong ones. Still, both strong and weak attitude followers can change
their attitudes to neutral independently at a constant rate. According to the theory of
multicolor contact process, if the propagation rate of strong attitude, compared with
that of the weak attitude, is large enough, the weak attitude may quickly die out ( Liu
and Wang 1994). This happens in the real stock market. If, on the contrary, the prop-
agation rate of strong attitude is small enough, the strong attitude and weak attitude
may coexist for some time.
More speciﬁcally, suppose that this stock consists of N + 1( N is large enough)
traders, who are located on a straight line {−N/2,..., 0, 1,..., N/2}, see Fig. 1b
where x-axis represents the sites of traders and y-axis denotes the time. The assump-
tion of the straight line structure here is to make the the structure as simple and clear
as possible, since this simplest structure is already able to generate simulation data
with most characteristics of interests. At the very beginning of each trading day, there
is a possibility p ( p > 0) of an emergence of traders with the strong attitude, and
we call p as the leader birth rate. Then, we select a certain fraction of traders (with
the initial distribution vθ2 ) randomly in the system as leaders of the market by setting
them as strong attitude holders. Assume that each trader can spread his attitude sev-
eral times at each day t = 1, 2,... . In each trading day, weak attitude holders acquire
some news, which is represented by a random variable ξt(θ1) from the standard uni-
form distribution on (−1, 1). Meanwhile, strong attitude holders also acquire some
different news by an uniformly distributed ξt(θ2) from (−1, 1), independent of ξt(θ1).
However, since strong attitude holders are much conﬁdent in their information, their
decisions on trading positions are more aggressive. Thus we suppose that their deci-

3.4 V olatility Clustering
One of the most important characteristics of a ﬁnancial time series is its volatility
evolvement (Bollerslev 1986; Bouchaud and Potters 2000 ; Cont 2001; Mantegna and
Stanley 1999; Samanidou et al. 2007; Wang 2007; Liao and Wang 2010). Most ﬁnan-
cial market time series exhibit volatility clustering, which means that periods of large
ﬂuctuations tend to be followed by periods of large ﬂuctuations, while periods of calm
tend to be followed by periods of calm. One quantitative manifestation of this fact is
that, while returns themselves are uncorrelated, absolute returns |Rt| (or their squares
R2
t ) display a positive, signiﬁcant and slowly decaying autocorrelation function C(τ)
= corr(|Rt|,|Rt+τ |) for the lag τ increasing. The plots of autocorrelation coefﬁcient
sequences of returns for the ﬁnancial model are given in Fig. 7. We observe clearly
the pattern changes of autocorrelation coefﬁcient sequences for absolute return series
with different birth rate values of p. When p = 0.5, Fig. 7a can hardly tell the dif-
ference between returns and absolute returns from the corresponding autocorrelation
sequence. As the p decreases, the difference between the ﬂuctuation patterns of these
123

622 D. Xiao et al.
two autocorrelation sequences grow more and more obvious: the one from absolute
returns is signiﬁcantly different from zero at ﬁrst but weakens then as the lag increas-
ing, while the other one does not display this decaying pattern, but falls generally
in the 95 % conﬁdence interval. It seems to take longer lag for the autocorrelation
sequence to decay into the conﬁdence interval as p gets smaller, see Fig. 7b–d. The
above results point to a link between the origin of volatility clustering and the new
leader birth mechanism in the proposed model. A possible explanation is that, when
strong attitude holders dominate the market, market behave more sensible to news and
consequently more likely to have large ﬂuctuations in price, meaning large volatilities
of returns. Therefore, when strong attitude holders dominate the market, we are likely
to observe the large volatility clustering together, while when weak ones dominate the
market, we may observe small volatility clustering. Each time when new leaders are
born, the fractions of strong and weak attitude holders are rearranged. This rearrange-
ment should not be too frequent (birth rate p should not be too high), so that the two
type of investors both have chance to dominate the market for a considerable period
through evolution process. This give an explanation to some extent, that the return time
series of the model show the volatility clustering phenomena when the parameters are
set at proper values.

4 Conclusion
Multicolor contact system is applied to a stock price model, in order to investigate
price ﬂuctuation dynamics for the stock market. We suppose that investment attitudes
are of different strength levels, and the propagation of different attitudes follows a
multicolor contact process. The dispersal of different attitudes is evolved according
to the interaction between neighbors at different rates, which is measured by propa-
gation rates λ1 and λ2. The birth of new leaders in the ﬁnancial market, controlled by
the birth rate parameter, results in attitude changes which will dominate the type of
market attitudes. Through computer simulation and numerical calculation, we study
the price dynamic behaviors with different parameter settings. The empirical results
show that, with a proper combination of parameters, the proposed model produces
123

624 D. Xiao et al.
qualitatively the similar distributions observed in empirical data: heavy tail, aggrega-
tional Gaussianity phenomena and volatility clustering. Furthermore, we introduce a
volatility duration analysis to investigate the intensity-duration relationship and the
duration dependence in volatility dynamics. The comparing results of real data and
simulation data also demonstrate the signiﬁcant similarity. These results show that the
proposed model may be helpful to the investigation of market dynamics.
