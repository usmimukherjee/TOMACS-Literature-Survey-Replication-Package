Abstract— Distribution operators can change consumer usage 
patterns through a variety of demand-side managemen t 
strategies, thanks to smart grid technology.  This entails the utility 
and the customers working together to modify customer loads in a 
way that benefits the stakeholders in various secto rs. The main 
challenge posed by system operators nowadays is cre ating 
simulations of the system market systems that effec tively choose 
generation resources under changing load patterns. An agent-
based bidding technique for markets is presented in  this article 
and implemented for a five-bus grid. The real power load model is 
created to enable connections to load buses from va rious load 
profile types, including commercial, industrial, and residential. A 
comprehensive DC OPF model serves as the basis for the 
Locational Marginal Prices (LMPs) and using Reinfor cement 
Learning, GenCos proactively modify their supply of fers over 
time. Two scenarios are compared relating the features of the day-
ahead market: one in which the load is connected as  a single, 
homogenous entity, and the other in which it is seg mented into 
various client kinds. 
Keywords— Agent-based modeling, Electricity market, Day-
ahead market, Sectored load, Independent System Operator, GenCo, 
Load Serving Entity, DC Optimal Power Flow, Locational Marginal 
Pricing, Reinforcement learning . 
I.  INTRODUCTION  
The operational aspects of the power system have se en 
tremendous change with the introduction of the elec tricity 
market. In conventional power networks, any variati on in 
demand is scheduled to be followed by the supply fr om 
committed generation units. Peak load periods might  have 
extremely high demand, necessitating the commitment  of 
additional generators. Due to this, the operators w ill need to 
spend more on procuring additional capacity, even though they 
may only use it occasionally during the year.  The recent rises 
in electricity prices serve as a harsh reminder of the potential 
benefits of major demand-side responses for the ref orm of the 
electricity system.  
Examining the combined offers from providers 
demonstrates that these price volatility extremes m ight be 
significantly reduced with even a small improvement  in 
demand elasticity [1]. In these markets, there is a n urgent need 
for significantly higher consumer engagement to imp rove 
system stability and lower price volatility. Planni ng, running, 
and controlling the power system will alter signifi cantly as a 
result of researching and simulating the diverse en ergy 
demands in the residential, commercial, and industrial sectors.  
The statistics show that the industry sector accounts for the 
largest part of load demand, with the residential sector claiming 
the second position and finally the commercial sect or. 
Analyzing electricity consumption sector-wise rathe r than as a 
single homogeneous entity, provides a comprehensive  
understanding of the diverse needs and behaviours o f 
consumers, enabling more effective policymaking, 
infrastructure planning, and resource allocation wi thin the 
energy system [1].  
Participants in the reorganized wholesale electricity markets 
frequently voice concerns about the opaqueness of p ricing and 
settlement procedures. In large part because of the  intricate 
nature of the market architecture in its numerous a ctual 
executions, market administrators’ operation manual s, and 
other public papers can be intimidating to read and  hard to 
understand. Furthermore, data is only posted partia lly and in a 
disguised form with a large time delay in several e nergy zones 
(like MISO). As a result, many players have doubts about the 
effectiveness, dependability, and equity of the fin al market 
results [2]. Agent-based modeling offers a viable way to model 
the electrical market with segmented customers. It is an 
appealing approach for researching how suppliers, c onsumers, 
and prosumers might behave and respond to the intri cate 
monetary, regulatory, and environmental conditions present in 
the electricity industry, as opposed to more conventional system 
modeling approaches. The efficacy of agent-based si mulation 
(ABS) techniques has been praised in the study of e lectrical 
systems and markets. When agents in an agent-based simulation 
model are allowed to interact with each other on oc casion, the 
simulation model can adapt and exhibit novel patterns [3].  
Consequently, one may be able to comprehend the 
dynamics of the bottom system. Entities favor machine learning 
because of its ability to handle large amounts of d ata and 
achieve convergence. It is highly recommended to use an agent-
based modeling technique to satisfy the needs of various market

II.  AGENT -BASED MODELLING OF DYNAMIC 5-BUS SYSTEM  
AMES V2.06, an open-source agent-based test bed, is  
utilized in this paper to simulate a wholesale power market. Li, 
Tesfatsion, and Mooney of Iowa State University pro posed an 
agent-based conceptual model, namely Agent-based Mo deling 
of Electricity Systems (AMES) [3]. AMES has four ma in 
components: ISO, markets, transmission grids, and t raders. 
Both buyers (load-serving entities) and sellers (ge nerators) are 
included in the trader agent.  
A day-ahead auction and a real-time market serve as  two 
settlements of market components. Specifically, for  the day-
ahead market, the AMES Independent System Operator 
(ISO) receives daily supply offers and demand bids from the 
generators (GenCos) and load-serving entities (LSEs ) of 
AMES, respectively. These daily reported bids and o ffers are 
used by the ISO to solve hourly DC optimum power fl ow 
problems by determining per-hour LMPs and commitmen t 
levels for the following day.  
Through reinforcement learning, the AMES generators  
modify their everyday reported supply offers based on their 
daily clearing amounts for the day-ahead market. Co ngestion 
cost components are incorporated into LMPs to contr ol 
transmission grid congestion. The foundation of the  study is a 
5-bus grid system, which is extensively used in ISO  NE/PJM 
training manuals [4]. Load profile is taken from [5].

III.  TRAINING SCHEMA OF GEN CO AGENTS  
Each GenCo  i upgrades the probabilities of action choice 
presently allocated to the sphere of supply proposa ls in action 
domain Ad i at the close of each day D by using stochastic 
reinforcement learning, accounting for its day-D se ttlement 
payout [11-13]. Stochastic reinforcement learning's  core tenet 
is that the likelihood of selecting a certain actio n should rise 
when the associated payback is reasonably good and fall when 
the associated payback is reasonably bad [14-16].  
For instance, GenCoi  increases the likelihood of selecting 
this supply offer on day D+1 if the supply offers i t reports on 
day D yield a reasonably fair reward, and vice vers a. Each 
GenCo uses Variant Roth-Erev (VRE) reinforcement le arning, 
a variation of the stochastic reinforcement learnin g algorithm, 
to calculate its supply offers, as described in [17 -20]. Fig. 2. 
shows the basic flow diagram of the process employe d, which 
is mathematically expressed below.

V.  CONCLUSION  
 This article describes network modeling with a cha ngeable 
load profile using AMES software. A dynamic five-bus system 
is employed as a test bed. The simulation's ability to account for 
various generation resource types can be readily mo dified by 
incorporating a time-varying load profile. The 5-bu s network 
simulation was performed using three distinct types  of load 
profiles: residential, commercial, and industrial. The created 
model took into account the location and timing of power 
injection to relieve congestion on a test bed, and an LMP-based 
pricing scheme was established. Using a powerful 
Reinforcement learning method, GenCo's agents were trained 
to bid strategically to sustain competitive market conditions. 
The outcomes show that the simulation is capable of accurately 
determining the actual power flow and losses into transmission 
lines. The findings of the simulation show that cha nging 
demand can alter the system's dynamic performance b y a 
considerably good percentage and profit improvisati on by the 
GenCos meeting the sectored loads when the learning  concept 
is introduced. Future work includes integrating an aggregator to 
make it easier for Distribution System Operators to comprehend 
what steps they must take to offer solutions for ne twork 
reliability.
