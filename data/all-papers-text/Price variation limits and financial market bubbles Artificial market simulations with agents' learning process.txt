Abstract-
Financial exchanges sometimes employ a "price variation 
limit", which restrict trades out of certain price ranges within 
certain time spans to avoid sudden large price fluctuations. 
We built an artificial market model implementing a learning 
process to replicate bubbles that has the continues double auction 
mechanism and investigated price variation limits. We surveyed 
an adequate limitation price range and an adequate limitation 
time span for the price variation limit and found a parameters' 
condition of the price variation limit to prevent bubbles. The 
price variation limits are expected to be an especially effective 
way to prevent bubbles, so the model should be able to replicate 
bubbles. When we gave a bubble-inducing trigger, which is a 
rapid increment of the fundamental value, a bubble occurred in 
the case in which the model implemented the learning process 
and did not occur in the case without the process. We also showed 
that a hazard rate enables verification of whether the models can 
replicate a bubble process or not. 
I. INTRODUCTION 
Financial exchanges sometimes employ a "price variation 
limit", which restrict trades out of certain price ranges within 
certain time spans to avoid sudden large price fluctuations. For 
example, Tokyo Stock Exchange employs two kinds of price 
variation limits that adopt different time spans: one is "daily 
price limit" which restricts price fluctuations within a single 
trading day, and the other is "special quote", which restricts 
within three minutes [1]. Most Asian stock exchanges (Tokyo, 
Taipei, Shanghai, Shenzhen, Seoul, and so on) employ the 
price variation limits, but many American and European stock 
exchanges do not because there is a debate over whether the 
price variation limit makes financial market more efficient or 
not. Because so many factors cause price formation in actual 
markets, an empirical study can not isolate a pure contribution 
of the price variation limit to price formation. Therefore, it 
is very difficult to discuss about a pure effect of the price 
variation limit only by results of empirical studies. Simulation 
study will help us to discuss about this very well. C. Yeh and

C. Yang investigated the effectiveness of price variation limits 
in an artificial market, and showed that price variation limits 
help to reduce volatility and price distortion [2]. However, 
no simulation studies have investigated an adequate limitation 
price range or an adequate limitation time span for the price 
variation limit in bubble situations. The price variation limits 
are expected to be especially effective to prevent bubbles, 
so simulation studies investigating the price variation limits 
should use artificial market models replicating bubbles. 
In many previous artificial market studies 1, the models are 
verified to see whether they can explain the stylized facts such 
as a fat-taiI2, volatility-clustering3, and so on. However, many 
complex models cannot explain larger numbers of stylized 
facts and/or are not more precise than simpler models [4]. 
Therefore, some have argued that the models should explain 
stylized facts as simply as possible in line with the "Keep It 
Simple Stupid" principle. In fact, very simple models such as 
[9], [10] explain main stylized facts. In these simple models, 
parameters of agents are fixed at the beginning of a simulation 
run and are never changed for the whole simulation run. The 
agents change neither investment strategy nor parameters of 
the strategy to earn better return using feedbacks of mar­
ket information; price change, trading volume, and so on. 
Hereinafter, we call such agents' changing "learning". "Non­
learning" models such as [9], [10] have been accepted as valid 
because the modes can explain main stylized facts. 
On the other hand, many artificial market models include 
the learning process [11]-[14] because many empirical studies 
have pointed out that the learning processes of investors are

In this study, we built an artificial market model imple­

B. Verification by hazard rate 
In the actual financial markets including bubbles, the prob­
ability that a run (sequence of observations of the same sign) 
of positive returns will end should decline with the length of 
the run i [20], [21]. A hazard rate H (i) is used for the test of 
bubbles. H(i) represents the conditional probability that a run 
ends at i, given that it lasts until i. For example, if there are 
100 samples that return positive runs i days and 40 times the 
next day after those samples are negative returns, the H(i) 
is 40%. Empirical studies show that H(i) decline with the 
length of run i if observation data include bubble phenomena 
[20], [21]. This means that the bubble returns tend to continue 
to be positive and this tendency becomes stronger as runs of 
positive returns become longer.

Next, we investigated optimization of flPpl, tpl. The price 
variation limit prevents bubbles but also causes converging 
speed of market prices to the fundamental value to go slower. 
The most efficient market, an ideal market, prevents bubbles 
and achieves immediate convergence to the new fundamental 
value. However, no market achieves both at once. To make 
amplitude of a bubble smaller, market prices converge to fun­
damental prices more slowly. On the other hand, if you speed 
up converging speed of market prices to the new fundamental 
value, occurrence of a bubble is unavoidable. Therefore, it is 
important to search for better parameters, flPpl, tpl, preventing 
bubbles and not trying to make the converging speed much 
slower.

V. CONCLUSION AND FUTURE STUDY 
We built an artificial market model implementing a learning 
process to replicate bubbles on the basis of the model of 
[10] and investigated a price variation limit. We surveyed an 
adequate limitation price range and an adequate limitation time 
span for the price variation limit and found a parameters' 
condition of the price variation limits to prevent bubbles. 
The price variation limits are expected to be an especially 
effective way to prevent bubbles, so the model should be able 
to replicate bubbles. When we gave a bubble-inducing trigger, 
which is a rapid increment of the fundamental value, a bubble 
occurred in the case with the model implementing the learning 
process but did not occur in the case without the process. We 
also derived a condition of parameters for the price variation 
limit to prevent bubbles. We emphasize that we found not 
only the price variation limits to prevent bubbles but also how 
parameters of the price variation limits to prevent bubbles. We 
also showed that a hazard rate enables verification of whether 
the models can replicate a bubble process or not.
